Standard Data Processing Agreement

Version 5 September 2026  ·  Blend Technologies LTD (United Kingdom)

This is the standard DPA that governs Blend Technologies' processing of end-user personal data on behalf of merchant clients. Merchants accept this DPA at the point of publishing a quiz.

1. Parties

  • Controller: the B2B client (e.g. supplement brand, gym chain) hosting the white-label quiz. Determines the purpose and means of processing end-user personal data.
  • Processor: Blend Technologies LTD, a company registered in England and Wales (company number 17236631), registered office 71-75 Shelton Street, Covent Garden, London, United Kingdom, WC2H 9JQ, contact contact@blend-technologies.com. Processes data on the Controller's documented instructions.

2. Subject matter and duration

The Processor processes Personal Data on behalf of the Controller for two purposes: (a) generating personalised supplement recommendations and nutritional analysis via the Blend-OS API (the primary purpose); and (b) in a pseudonymised form, debugging and aggregated statistical analysis to maintain and improve recommendation accuracy (the secondary purpose, governed by §6A). Processing duration matches the term of the underlying service agreement and the retention periods defined in §§6 and 6A.

3. Nature and purpose of processing

  • Automated decision-making: the Processor's algorithm scores supplements against the data subject's quiz answers and the Controller's product catalogue. Logic summary published at GET /privacy/notice. Right to explanation: see §10.
  • The Processor persists quiz submissions in a database (Supabase, EU region eu-west-1) for up to 12 months from submission, subject to the retention and erasure mechanisms described in §6. Quiz submissions are stored under row-level security policies scoped to the Controller's tenant ID; one Controller's data is never accessible to another Controller.

4. Types of personal data

Special category data under Article 9:

  • Demographics (age, gender, ethnicity)
  • Biometrics (height, weight)
  • Diagnosed medical conditions
  • Current medications
  • Pregnancy / breastfeeding status
  • Symptoms (sleep, GI, mood, cognitive, hair / skin / nails)
  • Lifestyle data (smoking, sun exposure, sport practice, diet)

Identifiers: user email (optional), quiz submission ID, B2B client (tenant) ID.

5. Categories of data subjects

End users of the Controller's white-label quiz.

6. Retention

Quiz submissions are retained for 12 months from the date of submission, then automatically deleted by a scheduled database function (migration 0039). Each deletion event is logged in an audit trail visible to the Controller on request. The data subject may request earlier erasure via POST /privacy/erasure (see §11); erasure requests are processed within 5 business days and recorded in the audit log. Marketing-use data (if the data subject opted in under migration 0040) follows the same 12-month retention unless the data subject withdraws marketing consent earlier, in which case it is purged within 5 business days.

6A. Secondary processing — service quality and algorithm improvement

In addition to the primary processing described in §§2-3, the Processor retains end-user quiz submissions in a pseudonymised form for two secondary purposes:

  1. Debugging — investigate user-reported issues, reproduce failures, validate contraindication alerts, and ensure service quality.
  2. Aggregated statistical analysis — compute non-identifying metrics (error rates, correlation studies) to improve recommendation accuracy across the platform.

Lawful basis (the Controller's): GDPR Article 6(1)(f) legitimate interests (service accuracy and safety) + Article 9(2)(j) statistical purposes with Article 89(1) safeguards. The Processor relies on UK DPA 2018 Schedule 1 Part 1 paragraph 4 (statistical/research purposes condition) and maintains an Appropriate Policy Document under paragraphs 38-41 of that Schedule.

Article 89(1) safeguards (Processor commitments)

  • Pseudonymisation: direct identifiers (user_email, user_name, IP address) are removed; quiz_submission ID is replaced by a one-way cryptographic hash; quiz_main (tenant ID) is retained for per-Controller isolation.
  • Minimisation: only health attributes and outputs necessary for debugging and statistical analysis are retained.
  • Purpose limitation — no cross-Controller sharing: one Controller's pseudonymised records are NEVER used to generate recommendations for, or made accessible to, any other Controller.
  • Access restriction: logs accessible only to the Processor's engineering and QA personnel under role-based access; no marketing, sales, or third-party access.
  • Security: encryption at rest (AES-256) and in transit (TLS 1.3); access logging.
  • No automated decisions about individuals (Article 22): pseudonymised records are used only for aggregate analysis and manual debugging.

Retention

  • Individual pseudonymised records: 90 calendar days from submission, then auto-purged (separate from the 12-month identifiable-record retention in §6; pseudonymisation happens at creation and these records are stored in a distinct logging table).
  • Aggregated statistics (no individual records): up to 2 years for ongoing algorithm validation.

Functional erasure on request: on receipt of an Article 17 erasure or Article 21 objection request, the Processor functionally deletes the corresponding pseudonymised record within 5 business days by overwriting the hash key.

Appropriate Policy Document: available to the Controller on written request within 10 business days.

Controller opt-out: the Controller may, on 30 days' written notice, opt out of contributing pseudonymised records to the aggregate dataset. Debugging retention may continue for the opted-out Controller's end users where necessary for service delivery; existing pseudonymised records from the opted-out Controller in the aggregate dataset are purged within 30 days.

Sub-processor (secondary processing): Render (US region; covered by EU-US Data Privacy Framework certification and/or Standard Contractual Clauses).

DPIA: the Processor has completed a Data Protection Impact Assessment under GDPR Article 35 for this secondary processing. Available to the Controller on written request.

Jurisdiction-specific note: the Article 9(2)(j) condition relies on UK DPA 2018 Sch. 1 ¶4. For Controllers established in jurisdictions with stricter national derogations (e.g. Germany BDSG §27, France LIL Art. 44, Italy Decree 101/2018 Art. 110, Spain LOPDGDD Art. 9), the Processor and Controller will agree per-tenant whether to (i) limit logging to the debugging purpose only, or (ii) require the Controller to obtain explicit end-user consent under Article 9(2)(a) for the statistical purpose.

7. Controller obligations

The Controller warrants that:

  • An Article 9(2)(a) lawful basis (explicit consent) has been obtained before the data is transmitted to the Processor's API.
  • A privacy notice covering the disclosures in §3-4 above is presented to the data subject before consent.
  • The Controller responds to data subject requests (access, rectification, erasure, portability) and forwards Processor-side actions (e.g. erasure of any persisted submissions) to the Processor within 72 hours of receipt.
  • The recommendation result is displayed to the data subject with a "not medical advice" disclaimer and prominent surfacing of any Verification: Caution flags returned by the API.

8. Processor obligations

  • Process Personal Data only on the documented instructions of the Controller.
  • Implement appropriate technical and organisational measures (TLS, per-tenant API keys with scope, rate limiting, encrypted disk on Render, atomic writes for catalogue files, audit logging via the observability layer).
  • Notify the Controller of any Personal Data breach without undue delay and in any case within 72 hours after becoming aware.
  • Assist the Controller with data subject rights (access, erasure, portability) within the contractual SLA.
  • Sub-processors disclosed in §9 below; new sub-processors require prior written notice.

10. Data subject rights — automated decision-making

The Processor surfaces the algorithm logic at GET /privacy/notice. Right to explanation is satisfied by the published parity status (PARITY_STATUS.md) and the verbose /recommend/v2 endpoint that returns each pipeline stage's intermediate output. Right to human intervention: the Controller may add a manual-review step on top of the recommendation result before display.

11. Erasure

The data subject may request erasure via POST /privacy/erasure with any of: user_email, quiz_submission, or quiz_main. The Processor deletes all matching records (identifiable quiz submissions in the primary database and pseudonymised logs in the debugging/statistical tables) within 5 business days and returns a receipt containing a unique request ID, timestamp, and count of records purged. Deletions are recorded in an audit log accessible to the Controller. Erasure requests override the standard 12-month retention period; records are purged immediately regardless of age. The audit log entry itself (containing only the request ID, timestamp, and purge count — no personal data) is retained for 7 years in accordance with UK financial record-keeping requirements.

12. Audit

The Controller may, at most once per twelve-month period and on 30 days' written notice, audit the Processor's compliance with this DPA under reasonable confidentiality terms. The Processor's standard right-to-audit response is a written summary of practices plus the nightly parity-guard / CI / observability artefacts.

13. Termination

On termination of the underlying service agreement, the Processor will delete all Controller-owned data (per-tenant product catalogue, tenant config) within 30 days unless the Controller requests return of a machine-readable export first. Logs containing pseudonymous identifiers follow the hosting provider's retention.

14. Governing law

This DPA is governed by the law of England and Wales and is subject to the exclusive jurisdiction of the courts of England and Wales, unless the underlying service agreement specifies otherwise. Nothing in this clause limits a data subject's or supervisory authority's rights under applicable mandatory data protection law.
