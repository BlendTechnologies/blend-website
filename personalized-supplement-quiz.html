Personalization vs segmentation

Persona quizzes don't actually personalize.

A persona-based quiz puts every customer into a bucket: “the athlete,” “the busy parent,” “the wellness seeker.” Each bucket maps to a fixed product set. That’s segmentation, not personalization. Two customers in the same bucket see the same recommendations — even if their goals, deficiencies, and constraints are completely different.

Blend scores every product in your catalog per customer, per session. Same answers always produce the same recommendations (deterministic, auditable). Different answers produce different scoring, with every pick carrying the specific signals that produced it — not just “you’re in segment X.”

For regulated supplement and OTC health categories, that auditability is how you defend your recommendations to customers, regulators, and partners.

Frequently asked questions

Algorithm questions.

How does the supplement quiz personalize recommendations?

It scores every product in your catalog against the customer using more than 30 product attributes (ingredients, dosing, interactions, evidence, contraindications, synergies) and more than 20 user signals (goals, sub-goals, conditions, lifestyle, and nutrient gaps inferred from a food-frequency questionnaire). Each pick comes with the specific reasons that produced it.

Is the quiz algorithm auditable?

Yes. The engine is fully deterministic — same input always produces the same output. Every recommendation carries the reasons that produced it. Important if you operate in a regulated category.

What kind of safety checks run on each recommendation?

Supplement-supplement interaction checks, medication conflict screens, upper-limit gating against tolerable intakes, and synergy pairing. Every flagged interaction or limit is traceable to a source rule.

How is this different from a tag-based persona quiz?

Persona-based quizzes and decision trees don't actually personalize — they segment. Blend computes a numeric, deterministic score per product per customer. Same input always produces the same output, every pick comes with reasons. See the main FAQ →
